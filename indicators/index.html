Step 1 · the auction

Price only moves when one side runs out of the other side's orders

Every trade needs a buyer and a seller. What matters is who is in a hurry. One side sends market orders and takes what is on offer. The other side waits with limit orders and lets the price come to them.

↑

Buy demand meets sell offers

Buyers in a hurry lift the sell offers sitting above price. While the offers at a price are bigger than the demand, price holds. When demand eats the last offer at that price, the next offer is higher — so price ticks up. Keep doing that and the candle closes up, above the area where sellers used to wait.

↓

Sell demand meets buy offers

Sellers in a hurry hit the buy orders resting below price. When sell demand eats the last buy order at a price, the next buyer is lower — so price ticks down. Keep doing that and the candle closes down, below the area where buyers used to defend.

A wick

Price visited a level, but the other side answered and pushed it back. The orders there were tested, not beaten.

A body close

The candle ended beyond the level. The market accepted the new price. The defenders there were absorbed or gave up.

A break that matters

Most closes are noise. A few happen at the exact place where control was being defended. Finding those closes is what the three engines do.

Think of it like a tug of war

The rope = price

It only moves when one team pulls harder than the other team can hold.

The marker on the ground = the level

Where the losing team made its last stand. That is where the MMM engines draw the origin of the move.

Crossing the marker = the break

The rope crossed the line and stayed there at the end of the candle. Control changed hands — or the winning team pulled further.

Step 2 · one language for all three

Two events tell the whole story

Every MMM indicator speaks the same words, so you can move between them without learning anything new. The letter at the end tells you which engine made the call: c candle, s swing, x zone.

The Flip

CDL · CDS

Control changes hands. The side that was defending the trend gets broken at the level it had to hold. CDL = buyers take over. CDS = sellers take over. It starts a new leg: T1.

The Continuation

LD · SD

The side in control proves it again. After a pullback, it breaks through the counter-attack and resumes. LD = long delivery, SD = short delivery. Each one adds a step to the leg: T2, T3…

BuPO4 / BePO4 · T#

The origin of a move — where the winning side started its push. It stays on the chart until price closes back through it.

P# · R#

The live range: the pullback the trend must break to continue, and the resumption base that becomes the next origin.

eBlock · iBlock

The zones born from a flip (eBlock) and from a continuation (iBlock). Each lives exactly as long as its origin.

🟢🟩 · 🔴🟥

Trend markers: where the current leg started and its newest step. Everything between them is always drawn.

Step 3 · three engines

Same auction. Three ways to read who is winning it.

Candle break engine

MMM Pro c

Reads control one candle at a time.

Every candle that closes beyond the one before it is a vote. MMM Pro c keeps score of those votes: the last time buyers forced a close, the last time sellers did, and which of those closes the other side failed to take back. It also separates urgent moves — when one side was in such a hurry that it left price the other side never got to trade — from normal ones.

When a candle closes through the level the trend had to defend, you get the flip. When the trend pushes through its own pullback, you get the continuation — each one numbered, each one with its origin and its zone.

  • ▸The earliest of the three to react
  • ▸Pullback and resumption levels, numbered by tier
  • ▸Urgency levels and structural voids
  • ▸Candle conviction colouring

Best for: precise, early entries on lower timeframes and traders who want to see every change in control.

Swing break engine

MMM Pro s

Reads control at the turning points.

A swing forms where one side's push ran dry and the other side answered with a close of its own. That turning point is where the losing side last defended — real orders sat there. MMM Pro s watches every swing, without filters, and waits for a body close through one.

A wick through a swing is a sweep: the orders there were taken, but the market refused the new price. A close through it is a break: the defenders were absorbed. Every break is graded by the order flow behind it — how much volume came in and whether delta (aggressive buying minus aggressive selling) agreed with the move.

  • ▸Break grade from volume and delta
  • ▸Strong sweeps marked separately
  • ▸Accelerator candles and un-auctioned prices
  • ▸Footprint delta on supported plans

Best for: traders who want clean, classic market structure with the order flow that proves each break.

Balance-zone break engine

MMM Pro x

Reads control between balance and imbalance.

Markets breathe. After a strong push, buyers and sellers usually agree on a fair price for a while and rotate inside a range — that is balance, or contraction. Then one side wins the argument and price leaves — that is imbalance, or expansion.

MMM Pro x maps those balance zones as boxes and colours them by who absorbed the most inside: accumulation, distribution or neutral. Only the boxes that hold a role in the trend — the pullback and the resumption — can make a break count. A body close out of the right box is a vote on value, not just on price.

  • ▸Balance boxes coloured by absorption
  • ▸Two box styles to test: growing or fixed
  • ▸Failed boxes cleaned up automatically
  • ▸The calmest, most selective of the three

Best for: patient traders who think in ranges and value areas and want fewer, bigger decisions.
