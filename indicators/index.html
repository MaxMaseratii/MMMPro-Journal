The philosophy

Price only moves when one side runs out of the other side's orders

Every trade needs a buyer and a seller. What matters is who is in a hurry. The urgent side sends market orders and takes what is on offer. The patient side waits with limit orders. MMM reads that fight from one simple thing: where each candle closes.

01

Demand meets offer

Buyers in a hurry lift the sell offers above price. When the last offer at a price is eaten, the next one is higher, so price ticks up. When both sides are equally in a hurry, price swings in a range: balance. When one side gives up, price leaves the range: imbalance.

02

A wick is a test, a close is a vote

A wick means price visited a level and the other side pushed it back: the orders there were tested, not beaten. A body close beyond the last candle means the market accepted the new price. The defenders were absorbed or gave up.

03

Most closes are noise

A few closes happen exactly where control was being defended: the origin of the last push, the edge of a balance zone, a swing, a fair value area. Finding those closes, and the zone each one leaves behind, is what the MMM engines do.

The MMM language · from one candle to one zone

Urgency, negotiation, fair value

MMM Candle Bias reads each candle. The engines read what the candles build together. A body close (BC) is urgency: expansion, one side is in a hurry. A 3-candle gap is extreme urgency. The NBCs after it are the negotiation, where the other side fills the urgent orders. A BC flip makes the turning point. The negotiation leaves a balance zone inside an expander block, and the fair value area is where the negotiation started: the best place for a reaction. Tap a point.

The options map · a different lens

Gamma Walls and Institutional Bias

The MMM engines read the candles. This one reads the options market and the big money. Options dealers must hedge, and their hedging builds walls: prices where moves often slow down or speed up. Every morning you paste the day’s levels from the free MMM Gamma page. The indicator draws them on your ES or NQ chart and tells you what kind of day it is.

Gamma Walls & Bias

Options walls · COT · open interest · footprint

Reads where the options market will push back. Big option positions sit at a few strikes. The dealers on the other side must buy and sell futures to stay hedged. Above the flip they trade against price, like a shock absorber: walls tend to hold. Below the flip they trade with price, like a gas pedal: breaks tend to run. The indicator puts those walls on your chart and adds what the big money is doing.

How it works
  1. Each morning, copy the day’s block from the free MMM Gamma page and paste it into Paste Daily Block. It holds today’s strikes (0DTE) and the 90-day walls.
  2. The indicator draws today’s call wall (the ceiling), put wall (the floor) and flip, plus the 90-day walls, zero gamma and the magnets. Index levels (SPX / NDX) are turned into ES / NQ prices for you.
  3. MARKET tells you the type of day: CALM when price is above the flip (walls tend to hold), FAST when it is below (breaks tend to run).
  4. BIAS reads the last month, week and day candles and checks yesterday’s open interest (new money in or out). MONEY adds the weekly COT: what asset managers and hedge funds are doing.
  5. At a wall a signal prints: Wall Held, Trap, Break or Fake Break, graded A, B or C by how many checks agree. Hover it to see where the idea is wrong and where the job is done.
  6. DO turns it all into one line, for example “Buy dips at floors” or “No bias · stand aside”.

Best for: ES / NQ traders who want the day’s ceilings and floors before the open, and want to know if it is a day to fade the walls or to follow the breaks.

Research only, not advice. The gamma signals are not part of the tested MMM strategies. Watch them on your own chart first.
