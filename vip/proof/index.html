Why I built the Prop Scale Planner

  1. I watched the interviews. These traders show huge prop payouts, and the channels verify them. So I took every payout as real.
  2. I tested their strategies. I coded each trader's own rules from their own words and tested them on 4 years of NQ, judging each year on its own. Most rules had a low win rate. Only 4 of 48 made money year after year.
  3. So I asked a better question: how do they get paid so much? I read 171 transcripts to find out. Their real strategy is not only the trading. It is the business side: many accounts, small strict losses, big risk only to pass, payouts as soon as the rules allow, and smart spending on evaluations.
  4. Then I built the planner. The Prop Scale Planner does that business math for you, with your own money and your firm's rules. And it adds the part that makes it stronger: a strategy with a tested edge.

How they turn trading into big payouts

Their payouts are real and verified by the channels. Here is how they get them, in their own words from the episodes, plus two simulations of the account rules.

Verified payouts

Chart Fanatics features traders with verified results, and we take every payout shown as real money. A payout total is the sum from many accounts, after all the evaluations it took to get them. That is the key: the payout comes from the whole account business, not from one trade setup alone.

source

What they do differently (their own words)

  • Many accounts. Apex allows 20, all copied with one click. Kane says he bought about 500 accounts; JJ Simon about 2,200 evaluations.
  • Tiny, strict losses. Okala always uses a 10 pt stop. Kane moves to break-even fast. Fabio has a daily max loss.
  • Bigger size when it works. Okala: "if you're hot, step on the gas." Kane rides news days (an $8k day on a 25k account). Fabio adds 3–4 positions on trend days.
  • Things the video does not show. Kane: "the discretion is my edge." Fabio reads order-book (MBO) data and says his model is not a standalone strategy. Candle data cannot test either.

The surprise: the prop deal itself pays

1,000 make-believe traders with no edge: 1 random trade a day (1:2, avg RR −0.05 after costs), copied onto 20 Apex-like accounts for 2 years ($25 eval + $79 activation, $2,000 payout cap, 6 payouts per account, rebuy when blown).

  • Median payouts: $96,000. Luckiest 1%: $392,000.
  • 80% ended up ahead after paying for evaluations.
  • Why: a blown account costs about $104, but a funded one can pay up to $12,000. Small cost, big possible payout. A big part of the edge is in the account rules, not only the chart.

Discipline is the real line

That fits the industry numbers: only 4–7% of prop buyers ever get paid. The guests are the ones who keep every loss small and run many accounts.

Big months come from size and copies

The host says Kane took $130k to $1.4M in about a month. Months like that come from bigger size on strong days, copied onto many funded accounts, with very strict loss control. Size is the lever; strict losses make it safe.

What you can copy

Their risk control and the many-accounts approach. Most of their entries did not beat random entries when tested on their own, so pair the method with a tested edge. Adding size into winners (Fabio and Okala both do it) is not tested yet.

Limits of this model

The prop model is simpler than the real rules. Real firms add consistency rules and payout reviews, so real numbers would be lower — and firms like Apex keep tightening caps for this reason.
